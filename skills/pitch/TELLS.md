# AI tells — the scrub checklist

Signs that a pitch sounds machine-written, each with the fix. The reference
is the Wikipedia "Signs of AI writing" field guide; the list below is its
pitch-document core. Descriptive, not mechanical — a tell that carries real
information may stay, but it must justify itself.

**The deeper principle**: these tells are all *regression to the mean* —
generic phrasing that could describe anything, chosen because it's
statistically safe. The cure is never a synonym swap; it's restoring the
specific fact the generic phrase smoothed over.

## The tells

1. **Rule-of-three stacks** — "fast, secure, and reliable" · "no account,
   no cloud, no tracking" · "spreadsheets, browser tabs, and your inbox".
   The signature AI cadence. Fix: cut to the two items that matter, or make
   it one sentence. A triple may survive only when it's the literal product
   scope ("remembers, finds, writes" for a tracker that remembers, finds,
   and writes).

2. **Reassurance tails** — "…so you don't have to" · "…so nothing gets
   lost" · "…so the AI knows what to look for". A trailing clause that
   explains why you should feel good. Fix: delete the clause; the sentence
   stands without it.

3. **Superficial participle tails** — "…, highlighting its importance" ·
   "…, all grounded in your profile" · "…, ensuring consistency". An -ing
   phrase bolted on to add unearned significance. Fix: delete or replace
   with the concrete mechanism.

4. **Smoothed generics** — "and more" · "and much more" · "various tools" ·
   "at a glance". A gesture at specificity without delivering it. Fix:
   enumerate the real list ("six built-in skills: emails, cover letters,
   interview prep, career summaries, resume keyword checks, grad-school
   statements"). The count should be the true count.

5. **Promotional register** — "vibrant", "seamless", "powerful",
   "effortless", "boasts", "rich", "beautifully". Travel-brochure tone.
   Fix: state the capability plainly and let the reader be impressed.

6. **Rhetorical questions as section openers** — "Sharing your resume with
   an AI?" Fix: declarative ("When you hand your resume to an AI, Waypoint
   can strip your phone number and email first.").

7. **Em-dash spray** — more than one or two per section reads as machine
   rhythm. Fix: periods, parentheses, or commas; keep the em-dash for the
   one aside that earns it.

8. **Significance puffery** — "a pivotal moment", "setting the stage for",
   "underscores its importance". Rare in READMEs but common in "About"
   sections and product pages. Fix: delete; the feature list is the
   argument.

9. **Stock phrase pairs** — "at your fingertips", "takes it to the next
   level", "game-changer". Fix: delete or state what actually changes.

10. **"Whether you're X or Y" openers** — audience-flattering parallel
    construction. Fix: name the audience once, in the pitch, and move on.

## The audit

Read the draft top to bottom hunting only these. For each hit, decide:
delete, enumerate the specifics, or justify (rare). Re-read after fixing —
the fixes must not introduce new tells (a rewritten triple of fixes is
still a triple).
